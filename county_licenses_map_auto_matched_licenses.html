Why jurisdiction boundaries matter

A 2026 report describes a Hochatown, Oklahoma lawsuit alleging that lodging taxes were routed to neighboring Broken Bow because Hochatown properties use Broken Bow’s ZIP code. The report says Hochatown collects approximately $4.35 million in lodging taxes annually, while the exact amount allegedly misdirected remains unknown. The map’s ZIP-code and jurisdiction-overlap tools are provided to help identify places where postal and government boundaries differ; they do not prove that any tax was routed incorrectly. Tax-collection context

  • South Carolina reporting says Airbnb agreed to a $60 million settlement with 16 communities over claims involving uncollected tourism taxes. Source
  • A 2020 Georgia lawsuit alleged that Airbnb failed to collect and remit occupancy taxes claimed by participating municipalities. These were allegations, not findings stated by this map. Source
  • Mission Local reports that Airbnb settled its lawsuit seeking about $120 million in San Francisco business-tax refunds for $0. Source

Airbnb states that it collects Georgia state, local sales, hotel-motel, and locally imposed occupancy taxes on qualifying reservations, and its general guidance says collection and remittance availability varies by jurisdiction. Even where Airbnb collects and remits taxes, this map cannot confirm property-level amounts or the receiving agency without sufficiently detailed public records.
